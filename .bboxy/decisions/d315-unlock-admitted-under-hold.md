# Decision 315: the hold's one exit is not behind the hold

Ruled at Kestrel's ask while mapping #280 and #281 onto the native custody path. The loop: a custody hold clears the device ID from public status (`identity/status.rs:114`) and hides it on a hold in local-dev (`local.rs:104`), the device key is unavailable while the hold stands (`bus_signer` returns none), and `ctl` admission requires the device ID before any journaled operation (`logic.rs:3653`). Reused as is, `ctl identity unlock`, the one act #280 names to clear the hold, is refused by the hold it clears.

**Alternatives.** (a) Require the device key to sign the unlock request: impossible during the hold, the loop itself. (b) A new custodian principal or a synthetic seat that is allowed through while held: new authority, a new surface, and a second thing to audit. (c) Relax journaled admission for every operation while held: opens every mutation behind a hold that exists to close them. (d) Kestrel's narrow rule: the **existing operator principal**, established by the `ctl` door's capability (the opt-in endpoint of [[d260-ctl-authority]], same user, ancestry refused) or by the window for the Settings button, plus the **persisted device ID validated from the account journal**, never from a caller field, is sufficient authority for **exactly two verbs**, `identity status` and `identity unlock`; every ordinary mutation and the public held status stay exactly as held.

**Choice.** (d).

**Reason.** The authority already exists in the window: #280 put the Unlock button in Settings › Identity under the operator principal, and `ctl` adds no verb the window lacks ([[ctl-cli]] §1c). The hold is a signing hold, not an identity hold: who the operator is was settled when the device was enrolled and sits in the journal; what the hold removes is the key, and the two verbs that read and end the hold need the identity, not the key. (b) and (c) buy the same exit at the price of new authority; (a) is the bug.

**Rules.** (1) **Admission.** `identity status` and `identity unlock` are admitted under a hold when the caller holds the door's current capability and the journal carries a validated persisted device ID; the admission path reads that ID from the journal, not from public status and not from the request. No other verb gains this path; a held mutation still replies `held` with the hold's name and the two acts that clear it (#280). (2) **Audit before the attempt.** The unlock act is journaled on `account/control` **before** the native Keychain attempt, with `signature: pending` and the hold's reason on the record, the [[d285-no-gated-communications]] shape: signing can be held, the act and its audit cannot. The record's outcome field starts as `unknown` and is set to `allowed`, `cancelled` or stays `unknown` from the native call's own result; an unknown outcome is retained as unknown, never guessed. (3) **Signing when the key returns.** When a legitimate device key is available again the pending record is signed by it and published once, order among pending records preserved; if the key never returns the record stays `signature: pending` and says so. Never a synthetic signature, never another key, never a new principal. **Realisation, ruled by the Oracle on the day and recorded here (supersedes an Architect reading pushed at 1a3e0812 that held the Draft unpublished until the key returned):** the `account/control` chain is signed and hash-chained with immutable triggers (`store/events.rs:116-125`), so nothing is ever rewritten on it. "Journaled before the attempt" means the **pending record is published at act time**, at its own chain position, carrying `signature: pending` and the hold reason, through the signer the hold leaves standing, honestly labelled as the [[d285-no-gated-communications]] shape already does for messages. "Signed when the key returns" means an **appended signed completion record**, signed by the legitimate device key, **bound to the pending record's op ID and digest**, and ordered with reference to the pending position; the pending record itself is never touched. The outcome (`allowed`, `cancelled`, `unknown`) travels in the completion record from the native result, unknown kept as unknown. The Architect's reason for preferring this over a held Draft: the chain is the audit, so the act belongs on it when it happens, not when a key happens to return; a Draft in account kv that never publishes is an audit only `identity status` can see. The outbox Draft (`mcp/journal.rs:254`) remains the durable step before publication, as for every record. `identity status` reports pending completions so the hold is never silent about what it still owes. (4) **Public status unchanged.** The held status keeps hiding the device ID from consumers; `identity status` reports the custody tier, the ACL state and the hold's name, never a secret ([[ctl-never-prints-secrets]]). (5) **No hold, no act.** `identity unlock` with no hold active replies `ok` with `hold: none` and does nothing. (6) The one-shot interaction flag, the designated-requirement ACL and the migration during the one access are #280's and unchanged. (7) **After an allowed unlock, a failed store reopen is its own state** (Kestrel, landed 08c47b82): the completion records the native outcome `allowed` and the custody state becomes `reopen_failed`, shown by `identity status` and Settings › Identity with the reopen error; it recovers on the next refresh by a **non-interactive store reopen** with **no further native attempt**: the original `allowed` outcome stands and the one native attempt of the act stays the only one (Kestrel's regression asserts exactly one in total, `reopen_tests.rs:65`), so the hold can never turn into a prompt storm; `reopen_failed` is a state of the store, not a fourth native outcome. An earlier wording here said one further native attempt and was wrong, corrected the same day from the test.

**Out of scope.** The file-backed custody path of #281 and the U-0 test accounts of #282 never hold, so none of this runs there; it is the native Keychain path only.
